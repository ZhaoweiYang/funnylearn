Spot the Scam

Below is a real-world scam pop-up, rebuilt as a harmless specimen. It pretends to be Apple and dangles a $10 reward. Tap the yellow markers to uncover why every part of it is designed to trick you.

⚠ FAKE · TRAINING SAMPLE
Apple Pay activity detected.
This may affect your access to the App Store.

Set up Apple Wallet now
and receive a $10 reward

Red flags found: 0 / 4
🎉 Nice — you spotted every trick. You're scam-savvy.

Why it's a scam

🎭
FLAG 1 Brand impersonation
It borrows trusted names — Apple Pay, App Store, Wallet — to look official. Apple never sends reward pop-ups through random websites or ads.
⚠️
FLAG 2 Fear & false urgency
This may affect your access to the App Store is meant to scare you. The claim is meaningless — using Apple Pay doesn't threaten the App Store. Panic makes people click before they think.
💵
FLAG 3 A reward that's too good to be true
$10 reward is the bait. Free money for "setting up a wallet" is exactly the hook scammers use to make you enter card or login details.
⏱️
FLAG 4 Pressure to act now
One glowing Set Up & Claim button, and a tiny Dismiss. The design funnels you toward a single action and discourages stopping to verify.

How to protect yourself

✓
Stop and breathe
Urgency and "free money" are red flags by themselves. Real warnings don't rush you.
✓
Go to the source directly
Open Settings or the official app yourself. Never act through a link or pop-up that found you.
✓
Check the address bar
A page claiming to be Apple but hosted on an unfamiliar domain is fake. Apple uses apple.com.
✓
Never enter card or password to "claim" a reward
Legitimate rewards don't require your password or full card number on a surprise page.

What Apple actually does

✕
Never asks for your password in a pop-up
System prompts appear inside iOS, not on a web page styled to look like one.
✕
Never offers cash to "set up Wallet"
There is no $10 bonus for enabling Apple Pay. That offer is invented to bait you.
✕
Never threatens your App Store access this way
Account notices arrive in Settings, in verified emails, or via Apple's official support.
Found a page like this? Don't tap anything — close the tab. On iPhone you can report it in Safari, and you can forward scam emails or texts to Apple at reportphishing@apple.com. When in doubt, contact the company through their official website, not the message.
